This Week's Notes

Why these numbers — what changed, what's holding, and why.

Window ICR ISF Trend Note
Breakfast (5–10 AM) RUNS HIGH 18g:1u 90 1:15 proposed Was the steadiest window in September (only 1 of 14 breakfasts ended above 180); now running high and getting worse. Same ~20 g breakfast every morning — counts confirmed accurate, no unlogged snacks or milk, not sick — but it needs more correction insulin each week: median 8 AM–noon corrections 0.26 u (Sep 28–Oct 4) -> 0.67 u (Oct 5–11) -> 1.49 u (Oct 12–18), with median morning peaks 207 -> 225 -> 272. Oct 12–18: 6 of 9 breakfasts ended above 180 at 4 h. Corrections underperform in this window (Oct 18: 2.3 u of corrections by 10:42 and BG still 299 at 11 AM), so the fix belongs in the meal ratio, not in more corrections. Bolus timing is already good — BG was flat or falling at the bolus on most mornings. PROPOSED: 1:18 -> 1:15 (not yet made on the pump — this field mirrors the pump). The 12 AM segment's carb ratio only affects meal boluses, so it does not change anything overnight. Do not change this segment's ISF 90, which also governs midnight–5 AM.
Lunch (10 AM – 2 PM) POST-LUNCH DIPS 20g:1u 90 1:20 working SYNCED 2026-10-18: the pump has run 1:20 since Sep 20, but this guide showed 1:24 until today. The change WORKED: lunches ending above 180 at 4 h fell from 13 of 20 (Sep 7–20) to 10 of 38, the median 4-h change went from +63 to +4, and the 2–5 PM block — which was mostly lunch carrying over — dropped from 60% to 27% of time above 180. Style dropped resistant -> caution and the RUNS HIGH badge was retired on that evidence. The cost: 3 short lows around 1:30 PM (58, 63 and 66 mg/dL; 10–15 min each, on Sep 25, Oct 4 and Oct 7), all on days when lunch was grazed with several boluses close together. Watch BG after lunch on grazing days. Keep 1:20; do not go stronger.
Afternoon (2–5 PM) 18g:1u 140 Holding Holding at 1:18 / ISF 1:140. With lunch fixed, the 2–5 PM block fell from 60% to 27% of time above 180 (median 188 -> 154), confirming its highs were lunch carrying over, not afternoon dosing. No afternoon snack ended below 80 or dipped below 70. Afternoon snacks score worse on paper (8 of 17 in range at 4 h), but the 4-h mark lands after the dinner bolus, so those numbers mostly measure dinner. Do not change this window.
Dinner (5–9 PM) RUNS HIGH 26g:1u 150 1:22 proposed Still the weakest meal ratio and the clearest change to make. Sep 21–Oct 18: 16 of 32 dinners ended above 180 at 4 h (median +26 mg/dL), none ended below 80, and none dipped below 70 — 0 of 48 dinners since Sep 7. 5–8 PM runs 46% of time above 180, 8–9 PM 52%, 9 PM–midnight 57%. PROPOSED: 1:26 -> 1:22 (not yet made on the pump — this field mirrors the pump). Fixing dinner also protects the night: big evening corrections are what drive the early-morning dips (see Night). Bolus before eating starts: median BG at the dinner bolus improved (184 -> 167) and fewer were given above 180 (55% -> 41%), but 13 of 32 dinners were bolused while BG was already climbing, and the Oct 17 dinner (46 g, bolused while rising) peaked at 350. Extended bolus is still effectively untested (3 dinner uses, each 35–40 min with 82–89% up front — none a 60/40 split over 2 h). Target BG for this segment is 130 on the pump, higher than the 110 used earlier in the day.
Night (9 PM+) EARLY-AM DIPS 26g:1u 150 Keep 9 PM sleep Values shown are the pump's 5 PM segment (1:26 / ISF 150 / target 130), which governs 9 PM–midnight. AFTER MIDNIGHT the pump switches to the 12 AM segment: ISF 90 and target 110, i.e. corrections become substantially stronger — a 2 AM correction using 150/130 will under-dose. Under-dosing is the safe direction, which is why the evening values are published here, but check the clock before correcting. 74 of the 77 events this period were corrections. Keep the 9 PM Sleep start. 9 PM–midnight still runs 57% of time above 180 (12–3 AM 27%), mostly dinner carrying over. The small hours dip: 7 of 27 nights went below 80 between 3:30 and 5:30 AM, and the Dexcom app shows 6 small overnight carb treatments since Oct 1. The dips follow evening manual corrections, not Control-IQ: nights with 1 u or more of manual corrections between 8 PM and 2 AM dipped below 80 on 4 of 7 nights vs 3 of 20 otherwise, while Control-IQ's overnight basal was the same (~1.2 u) on both. An evening correction rule is under review with the endo. Do NOT tighten anything that acts overnight.
